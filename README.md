# Euclidean Chains and Bézout Solutions

The extended Euclidean algorithm produces a particular solution of a Bézout equation. We study a class of signed Euclidean chains and show that, by choosing the signs and the remainders appropriately, one can obtain every solution. More precisely, each Euclidean chain induces an affine automorphism of the integer line that parametrizes the solutions of the initial Bézout equation. We prove that every affine automorphism of **Z** (the ring of integers) can be realized by a chain with prescribed first two terms and a final term of absolute value 1. The proof rests on a local classification: every step induces either the identity or one of two reflections, and these reflections generate the group of affine automorphisms of **Z**.
